# SOC pre-filter: your SIEM stops drowning

Your SIEM charges by ingest, and 99% of auth events are benign. This pipeline judges every authentication event at the edge — what threat pattern is this, does an analyst need to see it now — and only the escalations reach the SIEM.

## The problem

- SIEM ingest pricing punishes volume; auth logs are the biggest volume
- Analysts triage thousands of alerts; most are noise
- Threat patterns (impossible travel, credential stuffing) need *judgment*, not just rules

## How it works

```
POST /auth-events ──▶ Jev (1 call, 2 questions) ──▶ cascade
                                                        │
                    ┌───────────────┬───────────────────┴───────────────┐
                    ▼               ▼                                   ▼
              escalate ≥ 0.85   threat ≠ benign                   benign
              → SIEM            → warm storage                   → cold storage
                                  (scores attached)                (scores attached)
```

## The Jev questions

| Question | Type | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `threat` | Choice (benign / brute_force / credential_stuffing / impossible_travel / token_abuse / other) | Which attack pattern matches? |
| `escalate` | Noul | Does an analyst need this immediately? |

## The confidence cascade

- **siem** — escalate ≥ 0.85: straight to the SIEM, now
- **warm** — non-benign threat: warm storage with Jev's classification and scores attached, queryable for hunts
- **cold** — benign: cheap cold storage, scores attached for forensics

Nothing is dropped — every event keeps its Jev judgment, so a future hunt can re-examine the warm and cold tiers with full context.

## Cost math

SIEM ingest runs dollars per GB, while Jev judgment on an auth event costs roughly **$0.00001** at TypeSafe's published price — so the unit economics favor pre-filtering whenever most of your auth volume is benign. How much it saves depends on your benign-event ratio and your SIEM contract; the sample fixtures (5 events: 2 cold, 2 warm, 1 SIEM-bound) demonstrate the routing pattern against the included mock, not production savings.
